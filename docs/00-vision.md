# Vision

flowd helps a developer stay in flow. You decide how much effort each thing deserves today, track
the work with single keys and look back at the day in one glance.

## The problem

- Your day lives in your head and in scattered notes.
- Opening a notes app or a browser tab just to plan something small breaks your focus.
- Real days do not follow a schedule. Plans built from clock times are out of date by mid morning.
- At the end of the day you dig through notes to remember what really happened.

## Who it is for

- A developer on macOS or Linux who spends the day in the terminal.
- Someone who prefers the keyboard to the mouse.
- Someone who also works with an AI coding agent and wants it to read and update the plan when asked.
- It is not meant for teams, calendar sync, phones or reminders while the app is closed.

## Goals

- G1. Planning is a list of things and how long you want to spend on each, not a timetable.
- G2. Every action takes one key: start, pause, finish and skip.
- G3. The evening review fits on one screen and shows what got done and how much time went in.
- G4. Everything stays on your machine, works offline and opens instantly.

## Principles

Every idea for flowd is checked against these. One that does not fit is not built.

- P1. A day, not a backlog. Each day stands on its own, and nothing older than a week follows you.
- P2. Nothing happens by itself. No rollover, no reordering, no reminders. You decide, and flowd
  does exactly that.
- P3. Numbers are measured, never typed. Work and paused time come from what you tracked. A
  forgotten finish can be put right, but time that was not tracked is never made up.
- P4. One key, one screen. If a feature needs a manual, it does not belong.
- P5. A planner, not a project manager. No projects, tags, priorities, subtasks, recurring items,
  calendars, sync or notifications. An item too big for a day is split into two items.

## What success looks like

- You use it every day for 30 days, for work and study, and never go back to random notes.
- Planning takes a couple of minutes, tracking takes a keypress and review takes a few seconds.
- The numbers it shows are always true to what you actually did.
- You never lose a day, even if the app crashes or gets killed.

## Constraints

- Terminal first, on macOS and Linux.
- One local install, with your data in a single file.
- Times are shown in your local time and stored in UTC.
